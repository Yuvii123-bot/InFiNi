# Notices for the data in INFINI AI's built-in brain

The files in this directory are generated by `tools/brain/build.mjs`. They combine text written for
INFINI AI with material from the open datasets listed below. Every *reply* the brain gives was written
for this project; the open datasets supply example phrasings, word lists and common-sense statements.

| File | What it holds | Built from | Licence of the file |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dialogue.json` | conversation situations, the phrasings that trigger them, replies, jokes, riddles | this project's own text, plus example phrasings from Microsoft Personality Chat and CLINC150 | project text: same terms as the rest of INFINI AI; imported phrasings: MIT (Microsoft) and CC BY 3.0 (CLINC150), notices below |
| `commonsense.json` | everyday facts about things | this project's hand-written core, WordNet 3.1, ConceptNet 5.7 | **CC BY-SA 4.0** (share-alike, because it contains ConceptNet data) |
| `vocab.txt` | the words the typo corrector knows, most frequent first | the FrequencyWords list, plus the words of the files above | **CC BY-SA 4.0** (share-alike, because it is derived from FrequencyWords content) |
| `spell-guard.txt` | real but uncommon words that must never be "corrected" into a common one | word forms from WordNet 3.1 and the FrequencyWords list, plus a hand-written list of names | **CC BY-SA 4.0** (share-alike, as above; the WordNet notice below also applies) |
| `meta.json`, `build-report.txt` | counts and build diagnostics | — | — |

This work includes data from ConceptNet 5, which was compiled by the
Commonsense Computing Initiative. ConceptNet 5 is freely available under

http://conceptnet.io.

The included data was created by contributors to Commonsense Computing
projects, contributors to Wikimedia projects, DBPedia, OpenCyc, Games
with a Purpose, Princeton University's WordNet, Francis Bond's Open
Multilingual WordNet, and Jim Breen's JMDict.

(The paragraph above is the credit text ConceptNet asks for. ConceptNet's data licence is now the
Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence.)

- Version used: ConceptNet 5.7 assertions
  (https://s3.amazonaws.com/conceptnet/downloads/2019/edges/conceptnet-assertions-5.7.0.csv.gz).
- What was taken: English statements contributed to Open Mind Common Sense (`/s/contributor/omcs/…`,
  dataset `/d/conceptnet/4/en`) for the relations CapableOf, NotCapableOf, HasProperty, HasA, UsedFor,
  AtLocation and MadeOf.
- Changes made: statements were filtered (weight, agreement between wordings, removal of crude or
  personal sentences), reduced to short phrases and merged with this project's own hand-written facts,
  which take priority.
- Citation: Robyn Speer, Joshua Chin, and Catherine Havasi. 2017. "ConceptNet 5.5: An Open Multilingual
  Graph of General Knowledge." In proceedings of AAAI 31.

## CLINC150 (example phrasings)

Example user phrasings for some everyday requests (for example asking for a joke, the time, a coin flip,
or what the assistant can do) were imported from the CLINC150 dataset
(https://github.com/clinc/oos-eval), which is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 3.0
Unported licence (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). The phrasings were lower-cased,
normalised and de-duplicated. The dataset's out-of-scope utterances are used only as test material and
are not part of the deployed app.

Citation: Stefan Larson, Anish Mahendran, Joseph J. Peper, Christopher Clarke, Andrew Lee, Parker Hill,
Jonathan K. Kummerfeld, Kevin Leach, Michael A. Laurenzano, Lingjia Tang, and Jason Mars. 2019.
"An Evaluation Dataset for Intent Classification and Out-of-Scope Prediction." In Proceedings of
EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019. https://www.aclweb.org/anthology/D19-1131

## FrequencyWords (word-frequency list)

The vocabulary used by the typo corrector, and the filter that keeps rare words out of the common-sense
data, use the English 50,000-word frequency list from FrequencyWords by Hermit Dave
(https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords, `content/2018/en/en_50k.txt`), which is derived from the
OpenSubtitles 2018 corpus (http://www.opensubtitles.org/). The list's content is licensed under the
Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence. Changes made: only the words were kept, in
order of frequency (not their counts), filtered and merged with the words of the brain's own data.

## Rebuilding

`node tools/brain/build.mjs dialogue` rebuilds `dialogue.json`, `vocab.txt` and `spell-guard.txt` from
the sources in `tools/brain/`. Rebuilding the long tail of `commonsense.json`, and collecting the WordNet
words for `spell-guard.txt`, additionally needs the ConceptNet 5.7 assertions (filtered to English) and
the WordNet 3.1 database files in the directory named by the `BRAIN_RAW` environment variable
(`node tools/brain/fetch-raw.mjs` downloads them); without them the build keeps the long tail and the
guard words that are already in this directory.
