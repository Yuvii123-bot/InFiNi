## stories   (title | text)
The Lamp and the Sun | A small oil lamp once felt ashamed beside the sun. "I am so tiny," it said, "what use is my light?" That night the sun was gone, the village was dark, and a child lost on the hill saw one small flame in a window and walked home by it. The lamp never felt small again. Even a little light matters when it is the only one.
The Two Seeds | Two seeds lay side by side in the soil. The first said, "I want to grow!" and pushed its roots down and its shoot up, through stones and cold. The second said, "It's dark down there and windy up there. I'll wait." A hen scratching for food found the waiting seed and ate it. The first seed became a tree. Those who never risk growing are often lost anyway.
The Clever Crow | On a hot day a thirsty crow found a pot with a little water at the bottom, too low for its beak. It did not give up. One by one it dropped pebbles into the pot, and with each pebble the water rose. At last the crow drank its fill. Where strength fails, patience and thinking often succeed.
The Robot Who Collected Questions | A small robot lived in a library and had read every book, yet it felt it knew nothing new. So each morning it asked the first visitor one question: "What are you curious about today?" A girl wondered why the sky was blue; an old man wondered how bread rises. The robot looked each answer up, and by evening it had learned something it had never thought to ask itself. It decided the best thing to collect was not answers, but questions.
The Tortoise and the Hare | A hare laughed at a tortoise for being slow, so the tortoise challenged him to a race. The hare sprinted ahead, then, sure of winning, lay down for a nap. The tortoise never stopped. Step after steady step, it passed the sleeping hare and crossed the line first. Slow and steady can win the race.
The Farmer's Luck | A farmer's horse ran away. "Bad luck," said the neighbours. "Maybe," said the farmer. The horse returned with three wild horses. "Good luck!" they said. "Maybe." His son tried to ride one, fell and broke his leg. "Bad luck." "Maybe." Then soldiers came to take young men to war and left the injured son behind. It is hard to know, on the day, whether an event is good or bad.
